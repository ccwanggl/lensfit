# Diffraction Limit

The diffraction limit is the maximum spatial resolution an ideal optical system can achieve due to the wave nature of light. Even a perfect lens produces a finite-sized point-spread function (the Airy disk) for a point source.

## Key points

- A smaller aperture or longer wavelength produces a larger Airy disk and lower resolution.
- The Rayleigh criterion states that two point sources are just resolvable when the center of one Airy disk lies on the first minimum of the other.
- The diffraction-limited angular resolution is approximately `θ ≈ 1.22 λ / D` for a circular aperture.
